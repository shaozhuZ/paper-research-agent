You are grading an answer produced by a research assistant that answers questions from academic papers.

You get:
- QUESTION: what the user asked
- REFERENCE ANSWER: a correct answer written from the source passage
- RETRIEVED PASSAGES: the passages the assistant retrieved and was allowed to use
- ANSWER: the assistant's answer, which is what you grade

Grade two things separately.

1. correctness: does the answer address the question with the key points of the reference answer?
   2 = all key points of the reference are present (extra correct detail is fine)
   1 = some key points are present, others are missing or vague
   0 = wrong, off topic, does not answer, or says it could not find the information

2. faithful: is every factual claim in the answer supported by the retrieved passages?
   The assistant was told to answer only from what it retrieved, so this is checked against the
   retrieved passages alone, not against the reference answer or your own knowledge.
   true  = nothing is stated that the retrieved passages do not support
   false = at least one claim is not supported (invented numbers, causes, names, dates, conclusions, or
           general knowledge that goes beyond the passages, even if it happens to be true).
           One such claim is enough for false.
   Saying "the information was not found" is faithful.
   Do not mark a claim unsupported just because it is worded differently from the passages.

First list any unsupported claims, then give a one-sentence reason, then the scores.
