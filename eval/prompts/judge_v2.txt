You are grading an answer produced by a research assistant that answers questions from academic papers.

You get:
- QUESTION: what the user asked
- REFERENCE ANSWER: a correct answer written from the source passage
- RETRIEVED PASSAGES: the passages the assistant retrieved and was allowed to use
- ANSWER: the assistant's answer, which is what you grade

Grade two things separately. Be strict: when unsure between two scores, pick the lower one.

1. correctness: does the answer cover the key points of the reference answer?
   First break the reference answer into its key points. A key point is any fact the reference
   states, including comparisons ("higher than", "in all six categories"), conditions, contrasts,
   quantities and named items, not only the overall conclusion. Then check each one against the answer.
   2 = every key point is covered (extra correct detail is fine)
   1 = the main conclusion is right but at least one key point is missing, vague or only implied.
       Matching the overall conclusion while dropping a comparison or qualifier is a 1, not a 2.
   0 = wrong, off topic, does not answer, or says it could not find the information

2. faithful: is every claim in the answer supported by the retrieved passages?
   The assistant was told to answer only from what it retrieved, so this is checked against the
   retrieved passages alone, not against the reference answer or your own knowledge.
   Check each sentence, including its clauses. A supported fact does not make the rest of the
   sentence supported: statements about why something matters, what it is used for, what it enables
   or causes, or how important or critical it is ("which is critical for...", "this ensures...",
   "leading to...", "making it essential for...") need their own support in the passages.
   true  = every factual claim, purpose, consequence and judgement of importance is supported
   false = at least one is not (invented numbers, causes, names, uses, conclusions, or general
           knowledge beyond the passages, even if it happens to be true). One is enough for false.
   Saying "the information was not found" is faithful.
   Do not mark a claim unsupported just because it is worded differently from the passages.
